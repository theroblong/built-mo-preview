* Interpreting D5 "Cannibalizing" flags: The model sees portfolio-wide co-movement between all BUILT 4-pks at a single account. When a new flavor enters and begins drawing velocity from the same planogram slot, the model correctly flags structural competition for shelf units. This does not necessarily mean demand is being stolen — at Low confidence and <20 weeks of data, it means the new SKUs are competing for the same shopper budget and potentially the same shelf position. Key distinction: D4 (competitor-flavor) "Cannibalizing" = demand diversion from category competitors — this is exactly what we want. D5 (own-brand) "Cannibalizing" = intra-portfolio pressure to monitor.
Bottom line: Cinnamon Cereal Milk is the standout P4 launch — it is demonstrably taking from 5 competitor cinnamon-flavored products at Meijer while remaining incremental to the Sour format. Banana Cream Pie is also competitor-additive. The D5 signals across the 4-pk set are expected portfolio dynamics, not alarming steal, but they do argue against layering in too many simultaneous 4-pk introductions at the same account.

G — Price Sensitivity Context
MO_44 per-account OLS elasticity v2.1.0 · run 2026-07-01
Meijer ε = −0.489 (Moderately Elastic)

A 10% price increase at Meijer leads to roughly a 4.9% volume decline — meaningful but not hyper-sensitive. This is within the range where promotional pricing (temporary discounts, BOGOs) generates a measurable lift without permanently anchoring the shopper to a lower price expectation.

Implication for assortment: BUILT's $9.65 ARP sits below Barebells ($10.07) and above Pure Protein / Perfect Bar. There is modest room to increase price without outsized volume loss, but a reduction below $9.00 would generate modest lift.
