Each analysis module follows the same pattern

For every intelligence module in Mo, we identify the required data inputs and their source (SPINS point-of-sale, ERP, or POS), perform the operations needed to prepare that data, train a focused model, and display the result as two distinct layers: projection data (what the model forecasts) and context data (the live actuals that explain why).

FieldSourcePurpose

Operations performed on the data
Normalize price to price-per-bar across all pack sizes so single bars and 12-packs are comparable
Build 13-week regression windows per SKU × account — the minimum period needed for a stable price response estimate
Separate promotional price changes from everyday price changes, and flag windows where promotions overlap with price moves
Compare BUILT price-per-bar against same-flavor competitors in the same account and week to isolate competitive pressure
Compare price-per-bar across BUILT's own pack sizes of the same flavor to detect pack-ladder compression (e.g. 12pk priced too close to single bar)
Apply a guardrail that rejects regression windows where price moved more than 40% in a single week — typically a data artifact, not a real pricing event

04 Demand Velocity & Forecast "What will this SKU sell in the next 13 weeks, by retailer account?"
Data required
FieldSourcePurpose
52-week weekly units soldSPINSTraining history for demand patterns
Distribution (stores selling)SPINSNormalize velocity to exclude distribution gaps
Average retail priceSPINSCapture price-demand relationship in forecast
% weeks on promoSPINSSeparate promoted from non-promoted baseline
Retail accountSPINSTrain separate model per account — each retailer behaves differently
Week end dateSPINSDerive seasonality index and calendar features
Operations performed on the data
Normalize velocity to non-zero distribution weeks only — removing weeks when a SKU had no shelf presence eliminates false demand troughs from the training history
Compute rolling averages (4-week and 13-week) to smooth short-term noise while preserving trend signal
Build a 12-month seasonality index from category-level monthly raw averages (not model-estimated — directly observable from the data)
Flag promo weeks in the training history so the model learns baseline demand vs. promo-lifted demand separately
Train a separate forecast per SKU × retailer account — a SKU's velocity at Kroger is not a reliable predictor of its velocity at Target
Fall back to an exponential smoothing model for any SKU with fewer than 8 weeks of selling history

13-week demand forecast per SKU × retailer account

Category average velocity — how this SKU compares to the category norm for the same 13-week window

Operations performed on the data
Track weeks 1–16 of distribution build and velocity ramp for each new BUILT UPC
Compare each new SKU's ramp trajectory against the category norm for launches of the same pack type
Classify each new UPC: new pack size of an existing flavor, new flavor, or duplicate/relaunch (which determines whether it enters cannibalization scoring immediately or after a review)
Flag underperformance — SKUs where distribution build or velocity is tracking below the expected ramp curve at 4, 8, and 13 weeks post-launch
Suppress cannibalization risk scoring during the ramp period to avoid penalizing a new product for demand transfer that is still distribution-driven
