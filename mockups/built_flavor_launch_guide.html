When Should BUILT Introduce a New Flavor?
The core question: Does adding a new SKU grow total BUILT demand at that retailer — or just rearrange it? Cannibalizing competitors is the growth engine. Cannibalizing your own siblings is just shuffling the same demand. S&C — What Happened
Single-serve (30573) dropped from a Jul peak of ~6,000 u/wk to under 700 u/wk by Oct 2025 — the same month the 4pk ramped. The 4pk added ~5,400 u/wk but the single-serve lost ~3,500 u/wk. Net unit gain: +1,900 u/wk. In bar volume the picture is healthier: each 4pk unit = 4 bars, so 5,400 u/wk of 4pk = ~21,600 bars/wk — total bars/wk roughly doubled even as unit cannib registers.
Why It Cannibalizes
Both SKUs placed in the same Kroger stores. Same flavor, same bar size — shopper who bought single-serve now buys the 4pk. Model assigns 42% of 4pk demand to the single-serve donor (dist=1, prob=0.9996).
Better Path
Retire the single-serve at Kroger before or at 4pk launch. Use the 4pk to expand into new Kroger stores where S&C had no presence. Both moves keep total demand the same while removing internal conflict.
Mint Chip Counter-Example
Mint Chip 1pk (30432) kept growing from 12K → 18K u/wk AFTER the 4pk launched Jan 2025. The 4pk went to different stores (TDP 17% vs 1pk TDP 28%). No overlap = incremental. Select Mint Chip above to compare.

Decision Framework — When to Launch vs. When to Hold
✓ Launch — Signals That Indicate Incremental Demand
  • No own-brand SKU in the same flavor family at the target account (whitespace)
  • New format goes to different stores than the existing format (TDP complement, not overlap)
  • Existing siblings are in decline or being actively retired — 4pk replaces a phasing-out single
  • Category is underpenetrated at that retailer — BUILT has <15% ACV share
  • New flavor, not a pack-size variant of an existing one (flavor-adjacent, not pack-adjacent)
  • The new SKU targets a clearly different occasion (club bulk pack → Costco, not Kroger)
✗ Hold — Signals That Predict Internal Cannibalization
  • Same flavor already has strong ACV (>15%) at the target account in any format
  • New SKU is a pack-size variant (single → 4pk → 12pk) without a retirement plan for the old format
  • Three or more formats of the same flavor at the same account — almost always cannibalizes
  • 12-pack format at food/drug/mass retailers — multi-pack is a club format, not a grocery format
  • Simultaneous launch wave of 10+ SKUs — increases probability of mutual cannibalization
  • Existing sibling has stable or growing velocity — don't disrupt what's working
The Mint Chip Model (Gold Standard)
  • Single-serve first → build to 25%+ ACV and stable velocity
  • 4pk launch targeted to stores where single had NOT been placed
  • Result: single-serve grew 12K → 18K u/wk AFTER the 4pk launched
  • Cannib rate at Kroger Watch (30%) — acceptable with complementary store-level TDP
  • 12pk kept to club channel only → minimal conflict with food/drug
The S&C / 12pk Pattern (What to Avoid)
  • New format placed in same stores as existing format without retirement plan
  • S&C single-serve dropped 3,500 u/wk at Kroger within 8 weeks of 4pk launch
  • 12pk formats at Brownie Batter, Cookie Dough, S&C — cannibalizing for 97 weeks with no end signal
  • Seasonal SKUs offered in 2 formats at same account — compresses the seasonal window
  • Multiple chocolate flavor variants at same account → Milkshake and Brownie Batter compete

Base Velocity — The Benchmark Behind the Cannibalization Number
Base velocity is the stable weekly unit run rate of an existing SKU at a given account, excluding promotional spikes and seasonal surges. It is the anchor metric for interpreting cannibalization: a new SKU that adds 5,000 bars/wk while the existing portfolio loses 4,800 bars/wk is near-neutral in bar volume terms — the cannibalization rate looks high, but bar volume barely moved. The S&C example above shows this dynamic explicitly in the two-chart view: the unit chart (top) shows the 1pk declining; the bars chart (bottom) shows total bar volume growing, because each 4pk unit = 4 bars.
What To Ask
Did base velocity of the existing BUILT portfolio at this account increase, stay flat, or decline in the 12 weeks after launch? If flat or up → incremental. If down → the new SKU redistributed demand.
How Aevah Computes It
4-week rolling average of weekly units (or bars), calculated from SPINS data, excluding weeks flagged as promotional. The benchmark is the pre-launch plateau average for the 8 weeks before launch week.
So What / Now What
If base velocity of the portfolio is down → bring the bar chart above to the buyer and propose a planogram swap. If up → use the bar growth number in the next QBR as proof of incremental lift from the new SKU.
