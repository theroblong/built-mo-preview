When Should BUILT Introduce a New Flavor?
The core question: Does adding a new SKU grow total BUILT demand at that retailer — or just rearrange it? Cannibalizing competitors is the growth engine. Cannibalizing your own siblings is just shuffling the same demand. The analysis below covers all 38 BUILT UPCs with a confirmed cannibalizing signal across 12 accounts · SPINS MULO CRMA · Sep 2024 – Apr 2026.
Example: Strawberries N Cream at Kroger — Before & After 4pk Introduction Weekly units · SPINS MULO CRMA · Apr 2025 – Apr 2026
What Happened
Single-serve (30573) dropped from a Jul peak of ~6,000 u/wk to under 700 u/wk by Oct 2025 — the same month the 4pk ramped. The 4pk added ~5,400 u/wk but the single-serve lost ~3,500 u/wk. Net total S&C gain: +1,900 u/wk.
Why It Cannibalizes
Both SKUs are placed in the same Kroger stores. The 4pk is the same flavor and same size bar — a shopper who bought one single-serve now buys the 4pk. The model assigns 42% of 4pk demand to the single-serve donor (dist=1, prob=0.9996).
Better Path
Retire the single-serve at Kroger before or at 4pk launch. Use the 4pk to expand into new Kroger stores where S&C had no presence. Both moves would keep the total demand figure the same while removing the internal conflict.
Counter-Example (Mint Chip)
Mint Chip single-serve (30432) kept growing from 12,000 → 18,000 u/wk AFTER the 4pk launched in Jan 2025. Why? The 4pk went to different stores (TDP 17% vs single-serve TDP 28%). No overlap = incremental.

Decision Framework — When to Launch vs. When to Hold
✓ Launch — Signals That Indicate Incremental Demand
  • No own-brand SKU in the same flavor family at the target account (whitespace)
  • New format goes to different stores than the existing format (TDP complement, not overlap)
  • Existing siblings are in decline or being actively retired — 4pk replaces a phasing-out single
  • Category is underpenetrated at that retailer — BUILT has <15% ACV share
  • New flavor, not a pack-size variant of an existing one (flavor-adjacent, not pack-adjacent)
  • The new SKU targets a clearly different occasion (club bulk pack → Costco, not Kroger)
✗ Hold — Signals That Predict Internal Cannibalization
  • Same flavor already has strong ACV (>15%) at the target account in any format
  • New SKU is a pack-size variant (single → 4pk → 12pk) without a retirement plan for the old format
  • Three or more formats of the same flavor at the same account — almost always cannibalizes
  • 12-pack format at food/drug/mass retailers — multi-pack is a club format, not a grocery format
  • Simultaneous launch wave of 10+ SKUs — increases probability of mutual cannibalization
  • Existing sibling has stable or growing velocity — don't disrupt what's working
The Mint Chip Model (Gold Standard)
  • Single-serve first → build to 25%+ ACV and stable velocity
  • 4pk launch targeted to stores where single had NOT been placed
  • Result: single-serve grew 12K → 18K u/wk AFTER the 4pk launched
  • Cannib rate at Kroger Watch (30%) — acceptable with complementary store-level TDP
  • 12pk kept to club channel only → minimal conflict with food/drug
The S&C / 12pk Pattern (What to Avoid)
  • New format placed in same stores as existing format without retirement plan
  • S&C single-serve dropped 3,500 u/wk at Kroger within 8 weeks of 4pk launch
  • 12pk formats at Brownie Batter, Cookie Dough, S&C — cannibalizing for 97 weeks with no end signal
  • Seasonal SKUs offered in 2 formats at same account — compresses the seasonal window
  • Multiple chocolate flavor variants at same account → Milkshake and Brownie Batter compete

Minimize Cannibalization
Launch Apr–May. Category demand rising into summer attracts net-new buyers. Build distribution before the June–July peak so the SKU enters at full TDP when category is growing fastest.
Maximize Early Velocity
Launch Jan–Feb. New Year demand surge drives 1–4 week ramp to plateau — fastest in the dataset. Accept ~60% cannibalization as the cost of hitting early volume targets.
Quiet TDP Build
Launch Oct–Nov. Slow ramp (15–17 wks) through the trough. Lower pressure on early performance metrics, and the SKU enters Q1 peak with established distribution. Avoid December specifically.
Avoid: December
89% avg settled cannib rate — highest in the dataset. Every new SKU launched in Dec 2025 showed ~88% by week 9. The trough concentrates all demand among existing buyers with no new occasions to capture.

Ingredient availability, shelf reset cycles, and retailer promotional calendars may override demand timing in practice.
