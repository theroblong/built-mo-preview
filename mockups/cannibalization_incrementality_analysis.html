New Flavor Incrementality — When Is Adding a New Flavor Not Advised?
Two BUILT launches scored across Kroger + Walmart · Real data from Aevah's cannibalization model · Own-brand vs. competitive displacement distinguished
The Diagnostic Framework
Aevah scores every new SKU against both own-brand siblings and competitor SKUs. The critical question is not whether cannibalization is occurring — it always is at launch — but who is being displaced. A new BUILT flavor stealing from competitors is market share growth. A new BUILT flavor stealing from its own siblings is portfolio reshuffling. Own-brand donor count is the primary diagnostic. Rate trajectory is the confirmation.
Incremental
Own-brand donors: 0 · Rate settles <10%
New flavor enters genuinely new demand territory. Competitive displacement is occurring — which is the goal. No existing BUILT SKU is materially affected.
→ Approve distribution without offering a swap
Watch
Own-brand donors: 1–2 · Rate settles 10–25%
Some own-brand overlap exists. May reflect a planned format upgrade or a sibling SKU being phased out. Monitor the sibling's trajectory before committing to full distribution.
→ Approve; track sibling SKU velocity closely
Cannibalizing
Own-brand donors: multiple · Rate stays >25%
New flavor is primarily rearranging existing BUILT demand. Net portfolio volume will likely be flat or negative even if the new SKU posts strong individual numbers.
→ Proactively propose a swap or hold for more data

What Aevah Is Detecting
→ Donor (dist=1): Built Puff Strawberries N Cream 1.41oz single-serve — same flavor, single-serve format. Model prob = 0.9996. Velocity unchanged after 4pk launch.
→ 18 total donors in the weekly scoring pool. The dominant own-brand signal is the single-serve — this is a pack-size conflict, not a cross-flavor issue.
→ Rate plateau: Rolling avg never dips below 46% in the first 16 weeks. Healthy items settle below 10% by week 4. This one has not settled.
Recommended Action
Offer the buyer a planogram swap: replace the single-serve Strawberries N Cream with the 4pk at the same shelf position. This converts an internal conflict into a clean format upgrade — better value per unit for the shopper, preserved category real estate for BUILT, and a clean story for the buyer.
Decision Rules
Incremental  0 own-brand donors → add without swapping

Cannibalizing  1+ own-brand donors, rate >25% sustained → offer a swap

Application to Dreamwich + How This Becomes a Live Feature
What Aevah will tell the sales team for each Dreamwich flavor × account

Timing note: Dreamwich is planned for a June launch. Based on the seasonal analysis in the BUILT Flavor Launch Guide, June is estimated in the same favorable range as Apr–May (~20–30% settled cannib vs. ~12% for April, but directional due to limited June data). Launching in June positions Dreamwich to reach full TDP by the August–September peak — the strongest volume window in the category calendar. As long as Dreamwich enters genuinely new taste territory (which, as a sandwich-style product, it likely does), the own-brand donor count should be 0 or near-0 from day one.

Once Dreamwich SKUs appear in SPINS data, Aevah scores each one automatically on every weekly refresh — no setup required. The diagnostic the examples above demonstrate is the same one the model runs for every new SKU: own-brand donor count and the rate trajectory over the first 4–8 weeks. Flavors entering genuinely new taste territory show 0 own-brand donors immediately — a clean signal to bring to the buyer. Flavors adjacent to existing BUILT formats surface their own-brand overlap by name and probability, letting the rep proactively offer a trade-out before the buyer raises it.

The account-level difference shown above — the same flavor settling at different speeds at Kroger vs. Walmart — is also actionable: it tells the sales team which accounts are ready for distribution expansion and which need more runway.

So what / now what: Share this framework with the sales team before the June Dreamwich push. The verdict cards above are exactly what will appear in Mo's Brand & Launch module for each SKU × account. The next step is building that filterable UI experience — a retailer selector + flavor picker that renders the rate chart, donor count, and verdict for any combination on demand, updated weekly. If the first 4 weeks of Dreamwich data show 0 own-brand donors and a rate already declining from 100%, that is the signal to accelerate distribution expansion at that account.
