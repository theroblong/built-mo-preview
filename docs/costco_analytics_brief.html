Part 1 — Circana CRX

What is the CRX data and what does it cover?

The short answer: Circana CRX is BUILT's Costco point-of-sale feed — every item, every warehouse, every week since January 2023. It is the primary data source for Mo's Costco view and gives us demand velocity, distribution growth, and promo signal at warehouse-level granularity.
History 3.5 yrs Jan 2023 – Aug 2026
Warehouses 918 across 11 regions
Items Tracked 9 active Costco SKUs
Total Revenue $252M across full history

The feed is refreshed weekly by Circana (BUILT's data partner, coordinated through Justin Fisher). Each record represents one item at one warehouse for one week — giving us the granular detail needed for true warehouse-level analytics, not just national aggregates.

Mo ingests this feed automatically. The data is available inside the platform within the week it arrives and is aligned with SPINS national data so BUILT can compare Costco performance against conventional retail in a single view.

What this data enables in Mo

Four core capabilities unlock from CRX data. Each maps directly to an Experience 1 screen that the Mo platform will surface to BUILT's commercial team.
CAP 01

Demand Velocity (DPWPW)

Dollars Per Warehouse Per Week — the definitive Costco rate-of-sale metric. Normalizes revenue against the growing warehouse count so trend analysis is valid across the distribution build-out. Shows which items are accelerating or stalling. Feeds the Customer Scorecard tab.

CAP 02

Distribution Tracking

Warehouse-level sell-through, by region. How many Costco locations carry each item, where they are, and how their count has changed over time. Identifies distribution gaps vs. the 702 active US selling warehouses. Feeds the Distribution Tracker tab.

CAP 03

MVM / Promo Lift Analysis

Monthly Multi-Vendor Mailer and Instant Rebate Coupon events are auto-flagged in CRX scan data. Mo can quantify the lift an MVM event produces — baseline demand vs. promoted demand — and compare across events. Feeds promo planning within the Customer Scorecard.

CAP 04

Cross-Retailer Benchmarking

CRX is joined to SPINS data inside Mo, so BUILT can see Costco performance alongside all conventional retail accounts in the same view. Same filters, same time axis, same demand model. Feeds the Book of Business top-level summary.

What CRX covers — and what it doesn't

Capability checklist

  • ✓
    BUILT demand and velocity, weekly — by item, warehouse, region. Full history from 2023.
  • ✓
    Pricing — Average Retail Price derived from dollar ÷ unit sales each week.
  • ✓
    Distribution — Warehouses Selling as a proxy for TDP (as a % of 702 active US locations).
  • ✓
    MVM / Instant Rebate promo flag — auto-applied at register; captured in CRX scan data. Monthly coupon book events visible.
  • ✗
    Competitor data — CRX is brand-specific; only BUILT items are in the feed. No competitive benchmarking at Costco.
  • ✗
    Baseline / incremental units — No Circana-native base/incremental split. Mo proxies lift via event-period comparison.
  • ✗
    TPR and price-match promos — Only MVM/IRC shows in promo measures. Temporary price reductions and COMPs are not flagged.
  • ✗
    OOS and in-stock % — Fields exist in the data structure but Circana has not yet been able to populate them. Justin is following up.
  • ✗
    BUILT's recognized revenue and deductions — CRX is consumer scan data. Gross-to-Net (allowances, returns, spoils) comes from NetSuite, not Circana.

Part 2 — Ebad Meeting Prep

Why Mo needs NetSuite shipment data

The core gap: CRX shows what Costco sold to consumers. NetSuite shows what BUILT shipped and invoiced to Costco. Without both, Mo can report demand but not revenue — and the Gross-to-Net tab of Experience 1 is entirely blocked without NS2 data.

These two data streams are complementary, not redundant. CRX (sell-out) tells us consumer demand and velocity. NS2 (sell-in) tells us BUILT's actual shipments and recognized revenue. The gap between them — the sell-through spread — is one of the most operationally valuable signals in the platform.

Data source What it measures Direction Owner
Circana CRX Consumer purchases at Costco register Sell-out (demand) Justin Fisher
NetSuite NS2 BUILT invoices to Costco; bars shipped, deductions Sell-in (revenue) Ebad Hashemi

What insights unlock with shipment data

Gross-to-Net

Full revenue waterfall

Gross invoice → trade allowances → returns → spoils → net revenue. BUILT's finance team tracks this manually today. Mo connects it to demand data so you can see the Gross-to-Net alongside the velocity that drove it.

Sell-Through Rate

Are shipments turning?

Compare bars shipped (NS2) vs. bars scanned (CRX) on the same weekly axis. A widening gap = inventory building at Costco — an early warning sign before it becomes a returns problem.

Replenishment

Post-MVM reorder signal

After a promotional event depletes shelf stock, how quickly does Costco reorder and how large is the replenishment order? NS2 invoices answer this — CRX alone cannot.

Demand Forecasting

Forecast validation loop

Mo's demand forecasts predict consumer pull-through. NS2 shipments confirm whether Costco is ordering to plan. Divergence = early signal that the forecast needs recalibration or that Costco has changed its ordering pattern.

Lead Times

Ship-to-scan lag

Ship date in NS2 vs. scan date in CRX quantifies the lead time from BUILT warehouse to Costco shelf. Critical for accurate demand planning, especially going into high-velocity MVM weeks.

Trade Spend ROI

Cost-per-incremental-bar

NS2 captures all trade allowances paid to Costco. CRX captures the resulting lift. Together: true cost per incremental case sold — the finance metric that justifies (or challenges) MVM investment.

Questions to ask Ebad

Goal of the conversation: Understand what data is available in NS2, at what granularity, and agree on a delivery cadence. We are asking for a recurring data feed, not a one-time export.

Come prepared with context: Mo already has the NS2 schema documented (57 tables). We know the core query involves ns2.transaction + ns2.transactionLine filtered to Invoice type. We need Ebad to confirm the deduction and allowance line structure.

  1. Delivery cadence: Can NS2 invoice data be exported weekly, automated? We need weekly granularity to align with CRX. Is there a weekly batch job or scheduled report we can hook into?
  2. Customer filter: How is Costco represented in NS2 — one customer record, or multiple (e.g., Costco Wholesale, Costco Business Centers, separate for e-com)? We need to make sure we capture all Costco shipments.
  3. Deduction / allowance lines: How are trade allowances, returns, and spoils recorded — as separate transaction lines, as memo lines, or as credit memos? We need to trace gross to net at line level.
  4. Bars-per-line field: We believe custcolbars_per_line holds the bar-equivalized unit count per line. Can Ebad confirm this is the right field and that it is populated consistently?
  5. Item mapping: How are BUILT SKUs identified in NS2 — by internal item ID, by UPC, or both? We need to map NS2 items to the same UPCs used in CRX and SPINS.
  6. Historical depth: How far back does clean invoice history go in NS2? Ideally we want 2023-to-present to match the CRX history, but we need to know if there are any schema or migration breaks in the data.
  7. Access path: Is the preferred handoff a scheduled SQL export, a NetSuite saved search, a flat-file drop to MinIO, or an API? What does Ebad's team prefer to own?

What to leave Ebad with

The ask is one recurring weekly export: Invoice transactions for Costco (and any affiliated entities), with line-level detail including bars per line, unit price, and all deduction/allowance fields. Once it flows into Mo, the Gross-to-Net tab of Experience 1 becomes fully operational — connecting BUILT's recognized revenue directly to the demand and velocity data the commercial team already uses.
