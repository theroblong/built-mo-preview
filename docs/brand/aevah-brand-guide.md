# Aevah Brand Guide for Content Agents

Version 1.0 · 14 October 2026 · Derived from the live `aevah-com-website` repository (main @ `7a111a2`)

This guide is for AI agents and people producing Aevah content: articles, LinkedIn posts, decks, one-pagers, emails, social graphics, and image prompts. It condenses the website's source files into one brief. Image assets are in [`assets/`](assets/); see §11 for the index.

**When this guide and a source file disagree, the source file wins.** The controlling sources, in order:

1. Direct founder instruction.
2. [`docs/sovereign-ai-claims.md`](../sovereign-ai-claims.md): the approved Sovereign AI and hardware claims register.
3. `content/*.ts`: brand, commercial, buyer, solution, and offering copy as it ships on aevah.com.
4. [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md): positioning, tone, and review rules.

Do not settle a conflict by inventing a compromise. Open conflicts are listed in §13.

## 2. Voice and tone

**Confident, precise, commercially literate. Consequential without being grandiose.** Write for the executive who is accountable for the decision in this budget cycle, while keeping enough precision for the technical evaluator who reads next.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Name an active operating problem ("conflicting numbers in the same meeting") | Say "transform your business with AI" |
| Name the owner and the decision | Pile up platform nouns ("governed semantic orchestration fabric") |
| Show what changes in the work, before and after | Use fear-based AI messaging |
| State prerequisites, scope, and evidence plainly | Use unsupported superlatives ("world's first", "best-in-class") |
| Write short, distinct headlines with precise body copy | Tell headcount-reduction stories |
| Keep people in charge of consequential action | Imply that human judgment or accountability goes away |
| Treat models with respect, as valuable subsystems | Demean LLMs or frontier providers |
| Use plain language an executive can repeat | Leave technical jargon unexplained |

**Headline patterns used on the site:**
- Contrast then action: *Don't just predict the future. Test the decision.*
- Imperative and scoped: *Start with the decision that matters.* · *Bring your next decision.* · *Measure before expanding.*
- Three beats: *One decision. Three perspectives. Test the tradeoffs.* · *Proven once. Embedded everywhere. Compounding impact.*
- Ownership: *Built around your control.* · *Your teams retain the decision.*

**Mechanics**
- Use sentence case for headlines, buttons, and navigation.
- Use em dashes (—) without spaces, curly quotes and apostrophes (' "), and the serial comma.
- Use the middle dot (·) as a separator in eyebrows and metadata: `First Flight · How you start`.
- In display type, show emphasis by color (violet) on the closing clause, not italics.
- Use "customer" and "your team" rather than "users." Use "Aevah" rather than "we" in third-party contexts.
- Aevah is not a person. Never use "she" for Aevah or present the face mark as an assistant persona.

## 4. Audiences

Write for the person who is **accountable for the decision in the current budget cycle**. Before you write, answer: What are they accountable for? What problem do they face now? What would make a next step rational for them?

| COO and operating leaders | Coordination load, aging exceptions, decision delay, rework, skilled capacity returned to consequential work | Dashboards or headcount reduction |

## 5. Offers, engagement model, and commercial language

### Ways in (acquisition paths)
Enterprise operating-system transformation · Sovereign AI · Purchase of Aevah Private Infrastructure (Sovereign Rack) · Legacy MDM, Customer 360, catalog, and stewardship replacement · One bounded use case (for example Decision Pre-flight) · Technical diligence · Self-directed evaluation.

### Engagement names (use exactly as written)
| Name | What it is | Qualification you must keep |
|---|---|---|
| **Decision Working Session** *(see §13)* | A working conversation, using the Value Brief as the agenda, to test the consequence, owner, value case, prerequisites, and evidence | — |
| **First Flight** | A bounded implementation around one consequential operating area | Pre-packaged use cases **target** production within **30 days after agreed prerequisites are staged**. Custom scopes follow a separately agreed plan. This is not a universal delivery guarantee. |
| **90-day outcome decision** | Measures operating progress, adoption, readiness, and evidence to accept, refine, pause, or expand | Not a second implementation promise and not an ROI guarantee |
| **Success Capacity** | Flexible support hours the customer directs toward adoption, enablement, refinement, or the next use case | Builds internal capability and reduces long-term dependence on outside developers and consultants |
| **Next Flight** | An evidence-led decision to expand into the next operating area | — |

**Pricing:** outcome-aligned, negotiated against the expected value, use-case complexity, prerequisites, and Success Capacity required. There is no public list price. Principle: *Aevah only wins when the customer creates meaningful operating value.*

**Customer teams remain the operating owners.** Aevah works alongside existing staff.

### Calls to action
- **Talk to a person:** the HubSpot meeting link in `content/site.ts` (`HUBSPOT_MEETING_URL`). Never hardcode a different meeting URL. Labels in use: *Talk through your use case* · *Bring your next decision*.
- **Self-directed:** *Build your Value Brief* (`/assess`) · *Evaluate Aevah* (`/evaluate`).
- **Specific CTAs:** *Explore Decision Pre-flight* · *Explore the Sovereign Rack* · *Assess a replacement window* · *Review deployment options* · *See the engagement model* · *Begin technical diligence* · *Explore Security & Trust*.
- Avoid "Learn more," and don't send every reader straight to a calendar.

## 6. Sovereign AI and hardware: approved claims

Sovereign AI is a leading differentiator. These positions are **founder-approved (3 Aug 2026)** and may be stated directly:

- Every deployment option is available: customer cloud, customer-controlled inference, Aevah-managed private AI, Aevah datacenter, and customer datacenter.
- The complete on-premises offering is production-ready.
- Aevah supports every model and inference provider.
- Automatic model portability preserves the Aevah operating layer and the customer's Operating DNA.
- Air-gapped operation is universal.
- Security enforcement is independently validated.
- Performance, scale, recovery, and implementation guarantees are contract-backed **for the agreed operating scope**.
- Regulatory certifications.
- Complete rebuild, recovery, operational handoff, and customer-run readiness.

**Aevah Private Infrastructure** is sold directly as a complete, customer-owned system: enterprise servers, GPU compute, storage, networking, data infrastructure, the full Aevah platform and applications, model and inference operation, configuration, delivery, installation, hardening, validation, handoff, recovery, and support. It runs behind the customer's firewall or air-gapped. The rack is the primary delivery form, not the boundary of the offering.

**Sovereignty dimensions:** Data · Model · Compute · Deployment · Operational.
**Control stack:** Control your process · Control your data · Control your inference · Control your economics.

**Never invent** certification names, issuing bodies, validation firms, report titles, benchmark results, SLAs, component specifications, rack capacities, recovery times, or warranty language. These details have not been published yet.

## 7. Claims and evidence discipline

Every meaningful claim must fit one of these categories: **approved public position** · **approved evidence** (scoped, sourced, dated) · **bounded demonstration** (not a production outcome) · **customer-specific hypothesis** · **working direction** (not publishable as fact).

Hard rules:
- **No fictional customers, quotes, metrics, logos, partners, or certifications.**
- **No generic ROI or productivity percentages.** Don't infer ROI, scale, speed, or regulatory coverage from adjacent evidence.
- **Treat older blog posts as unverified.** All 35 migrated articles are `review-required`. Do not reuse their statistics, such as "3–5% margin recovery," "40% faster cash cycles," "$890 billion," "86% of enterprises," or "MDM by Christmas," without editorial verification.
- **The Proof area is currently hidden** (`SHOW_PROOF = false`) because the evidence library contains only synthetic and lab-scoped records. Do not cite `/proof` pages or present lab results as customer outcomes.
- **Label Decision Pre-flight numbers as illustrative.** The homepage scenario uses synthetic figures in a transparent formula. If you reuse them, label them as an illustration.
- **Customer logos:** use only the logos in `content/customers.ts` and `public/images/customers/`, and only where the website already uses them.

## 8. Visual identity

The overall feel is premium, editorial, calm, intelligent, and enterprise-ready.

### 8.1 Logo
The logo is the **face mark** (a woman's profile traced with circuitry, in violet tones) inside a lilac-ringed disc, paired with the **"Aevah" wordmark set in Newsreader Bold** (700, tracking −0.04em, Plum Deep).

| Asset | Use |
|---|---|
| `assets/generated/logo-lockup-light-transparent.png` | Default lockup on light backgrounds |
| `assets/generated/logo-lockup-on-paper.png` | Lockup on Warm Paper |
| `assets/generated/logo-lockup-on-plum.png` | Reversed lockup on Plum Deep (white wordmark) |
| `assets/generated/logo-mark-disc-512.png` / `-180.png` | Avatar, favicon, app icon, small spaces |
| `assets/reference/aevah-face-mark-transparent.png` | Uncropped face (1080²), for hero art and watermarks |

**Logo rules**
- Keep clear space of at least the disc's radius on every side. The lockup gap between disc and wordmark is about 0.24× the disc diameter.
- Don't recolor, outline, stretch, rotate, or add effects to the face. Don't crop the face's nose or chin.
- Don't animate the face into a talking or assistant persona, and don't give it dialogue.
- Watermark use: place the face large at the right edge, partly faded and masked (about 22% opacity on the site). Use it on light or plum surfaces, never behind body text.
- **Don't use the script "Aevah" logo** (yellow "A", purple script, `public/images/Aevah Logo Transparent.svg` and `aevah-name.jpeg`). It is a legacy mark that does not appear on the current site (§13).

### 8.2 Color
See `assets/generated/color-palette.png`.

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Plum Deep | `#170A2A` | Darkest surfaces, utility bar, wordmark, dark-register backgrounds |
| Plum | `#462453` | Primary buttons, links, key headings (hover `#643970`) |
| Violet | `#77548E` | Eyebrows, emphasis clause in headlines, accents |
| Footer Plum | `#38223F` | Footer and dark bands |
| Warm Paper | `#FFFDFA` | Default background. Use it instead of pure white. |
| Lilac Soft | `#F5F2F6` | Tinted sections |
| Lilac | `#EFE8F2` | CTA bands, mark disc |
| Rule | `#E2D8E5` | Borders and dividers |
| Ink | `#29232E` | Body text and headlines |
| Ink Soft | `#514758` | Lead paragraphs |
| Muted | `#625A69` | Captions and secondary copy |
| Amber | `#F5A65B` | **The single value signal**: glow, selection, one highlight per composition |
| Warm Amber | `#F4B45F` | Decision Pre-flight accent and CTA fill |
| Amber Soft / Gold Soft | `#F9E9C9` / `#FBEDD8` | Pale amber panels |
| Amber Deep | `#AC793E` | Focus ring; amber-toned text on light backgrounds (large text only) |
| Footer Amber | `#EDCF9C` | Small headings on Footer Plum |

**Proportion:** about 70% paper and lilac, 25% plum and violet, and no more than 5% amber. Amber marks the outcome or value moment. It is never a second brand color.

**Contrast (WCAG, on Warm Paper):** Ink 15:1 · Plum 12.6:1 · Ink Soft 8.7:1 · Muted 6.5:1 · Violet 6.0:1. All pass for body text. **Amber is 2.0:1, so never put amber text on light backgrounds.** Amber Deep is 3.7:1 (large text only). On Plum Deep, Amber is 9.4:1 and white is far above that, so both work well on dark backgrounds. For a dark label on a Warm Amber button, use Plum Deep (10.4:1).

Gradients are used sparingly. The hero wash runs `#FBF8F4 → #F8F2FB → #EFE4F5` at 135°, with a soft amber glow (≈28%) and a violet glow (≈20%) toward the upper right. Approved background files are in `assets/generated/bg-*.png`.

**Don't** introduce teal, citrus, bright blue, or neon as UI colors. A citrus accent was trialled and rejected in September 2026. Teal appears only inside the 3D diorama illustrations (§9).

### 8.3 Typography
See `assets/generated/typography-specimen.png`. Both fonts are open-source variable fonts (Google Fonts: **Newsreader**, **Inter**). Fallbacks are Baskerville/Georgia for the serif and Avenir/Segoe UI/Helvetica/Arial for the sans.

| Role | Face | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Wordmark | Newsreader | 700, tracking −0.04em |
| Page H1 / hero | **Inter** | 540, tracking −0.05em, leading 1.05–1.08, max ~21ch |
| Section H2, editorial display | **Newsreader** | 450–500, tracking −0.035em, leading 1.1 |
| Card H3 | Inter | 550, leading 1.3 |
| Eyebrow | Inter | 650–800, Violet, tracking +0.055em (sentence case) to +0.13em (uppercase) |
| Body / lead | Inter | 400, 16–18px, leading 1.65–1.75, Ink Soft, max ~68ch |
| Minimum size | — | 14px anywhere, including diagram labels |

The current site pairs a **sans hero H1** with **serif section headings**. Pick one family per headline. Don't mix serif and sans within a single headline.

### 8.4 Layout and components
- Content width up to 1240px, with a narrow column of 820px for reading.
- Use generous whitespace. Sections are padded about 84px vertically on desktop and about 48px on mobile.
- Corners: buttons 7px, cards 10–12px, panels up to 18px.
- Cards use a Warm Paper fill, a 1px Rule border, and little or no shadow (`0 22px 70px rgba(38,18,60,.12)` at most).
- Each card or block has one strong promise, one supporting explanation, one primary CTA, and one quieter secondary CTA.
- Motion must clarify sequence, state, or choice, and must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Any rotating content needs controls and a pause.

## 9. Imagery

### 9.1 The hard constraint
**Never show or invent product screens, proprietary interfaces, dashboards, detailed orchestration flows, prompts, schemas, or implementation mechanics.** The owner wants to reduce the risk of competitors copying the product. Conceptual diagrams only.

### 9.2 The three registers

**A. Light conceptual diagrams** (`assets/generated/style-board-light-diagrams.png`)
Frosted-glass and porcelain 3D tiles on a near-white lilac field, with soft violet line work and arrows, a single amber pulse for the outcome, and occasional teal for evidence. Labels are short (one or two words) in a clean sans. They suit explaining relationships: Operating DNA, before/after, evidence thread, deployment continuum.
*References:* `diagram-04-one-decision-advantage.jpg`, `diagram-operating-dna.jpg`, `diagram-06-before-after-executive.jpg`.

**B. Editorial 3D dioramas** (`assets/generated/style-board-illustration-and-dark.png`, top two rows)
Museum-quality architectural models made of warm white plaster, pale stone, frosted glass, and brushed aluminum, with navy, teal, and soft lavender plus one amber value signal. Clean daylight. Small, refined business figures convey ownership and scale. They suit offering heroes and value stories.
*References:* `diorama-first-flight.jpg`, `diorama-legacy-replacement.jpg`, `diorama-sovereign-ai.jpg`.

**C. Dark sovereign register**
Plum Deep and near-black backgrounds with violet neon line work and amber for the continuity or value path. Uppercase tracked labels. Use it for Sovereign AI, continuity, and hardware, and use it sparingly.
*References:* `dark-sovereign-continuity.jpg`, `dark-ai-capacity-portfolio.jpg`, `hardware-sovereign-rack.jpg`.

**Hardware:** `hardware-sovereign-rack.jpg` (dark, from `public/images/aevah-sovereign-rack.png`) is the approved rack visual. `diorama-private-infrastructure.jpg` is the approved scene of the complete installation. Don't generate racks with invented specifications, logos, or capacity labels.

### 9.3 Image-generation prompt template
This is the approved shared direction (from `docs/image-prompts/offerings-gateway.md`). Prepend it to any diorama prompt:

```text
Asset type: premium enterprise illustration for Aevah.
Create a brand-new scene from scratch with no reference image.
Visual system: sophisticated light 3D editorial diorama, like a museum-quality architectural business model made from warm white plaster, pale stone, frosted glass, brushed aluminum, navy, teal, soft lavender, and one restrained amber value signal. Clean daylight, soft realistic shadows, tactile materials, calm and credible—not a photograph and not science fiction. People, if present, are small refined business figures used only to clarify ownership and scale; the value-producing system is the hero.
Composition: wide 3:2 landscape, viewed from slightly above. One strong central silhouette; keep the essential value story in the central 65 percent. Keep the lowest 22 percent visually simple if copy will overlay it.
Constraints: no words, labels, numbers, logos, watermarks, software screens, dashboards, charts floating in space, generic holograms, padlocks, shields, keys, clouds, robots, brains, magic wands, currency symbols, handshakes, provider branding, or certification implications. The image must communicate the customer value, not merely the technology mechanism.
```
Then add: **Offering**, **Customer value to communicate**, **Scene**, and **Value moment** (what the buyer should feel).

For **light diagrams**, use this direction instead: "clean conceptual diagram on a near-white lilac background (#F7F4FA); frosted glass and porcelain rounded tiles; violet (#77548E) connectors and arrows; one amber (#F5A65B) highlight for the outcome; short sentence-case labels in a clean geometric sans; no screens, no UI, no data tables; generous whitespace; 16:9." Check every label for spelling, and keep text to a minimum. Image models misspell text, so add headlines afterward in real type where you can.

### 9.4 Imagery to avoid
Robots, glowing brains, generic holograms, binary rain, padlocks, and shield clichés (the existing dark diagrams use a shield sparingly, so don't add more) · stock-photo collages · handshakes · rockets and finish lines · invented UIs and dashboards · workers depicted as targets for replacement · fake customer logos.

## 13. Open decisions for the owner

These conflicts exist in the current sources. Agents should follow the stated default until the owner decides.

1. **Name of the discovery meeting.** `CLAUDE.md` says *Outcome Working Session*. The live `content/commercial.ts` says *Decision Working Session*. **Default: Decision Working Session** (what ships today).
2. **Lead line.** The site uses three layers: category *Enterprise Intelligence OS* (metadata, platform), positioning *The operating system for how your business gets work done* (`CLAUDE.md`), and campaign *Don't just predict the future. Test the decision.* (homepage). **Default:** use the campaign line for decision and finance content, the positioning line for executive and transformation content, and the category name for descriptive and SEO contexts.
3. **First Flight duration.** `skills/aevah-platform/SKILL.md` describes First Flight as a "focused 90-day evaluation and implementation." Commercial copy says a 30-day target for pre-packaged scopes plus a 90-day outcome decision. **Default: the commercial copy.** The skill file should be updated.
4. **Primary CTA.** `CLAUDE.md` names *Compare notes / Outcome Working Session* as the primary CTA. The live site's primary is *Build your Value Brief* plus *Talk through your use case*. **Default: the live site.**
5. **Legacy script logo.** The script "Aevah" (gold A) files still exist in `public/images/` but aren't used. **Default: don't use it.** Confirm whether it should be formally retired.
6. **Priority industries.** `CLAUDE.md` lists three, while navigation shows six. **Default:** all six are valid, with CPG, manufacturing, and enterprise technology getting the deepest treatment.
