Meijer Singles at Checkout

What other retailers show, what it could mean at Meijer, and how to test it

Add-on to the Meijer Account Brief v2 · Data: SPINS weekly, each retailer's own stores, through Sept 6, 2026 · Period: last 13 weeks (June 14 – Sept 6, 2026) unless a table says otherwise

Issued October 13, 2026 · prepared by Aevah (Mo) for BUILT

Summary

  • Meijer is the only grocery account that carries BUILT 4-packs but no singles. Every other grocer started with singles. The only retailers that added singles to an established 4-pack set are Target and Walmart, both supercenters like Meijer.
  • Both run singles as seasonal programs: in most stores for a few months, then back to a core of stores. Target put singles in roughly 70% of its stores for the 2025 holidays and in most stores again this summer.
  • When Target added singles for the 2025 holidays, most of the singles bars were additional sales for BUILT. Its 4-packs sold somewhat less than expected, but the size cannot be pinned down because Target added four new 4-pack flavors at the same time. Depending on the comparison, between none and just under half of the singles bars came out of 4-packs.
  • A typical grocer sells about 3.4 singles per item per store per week, a typical convenience chain about 2.0 (Wawa 4.5), and Target about 5 with singles in most stores. Singles sell for about 13% more per bar than 4-packs.
  • At Meijer, 4 checkout singles in all stores could sell roughly 2,240–5,376 bars a week (scenarios below). Even if close to half came out of 4-packs, BUILT would gain bars and dollars. This is a scenario, not a forecast.
  • Recommended: a 13-week test in a set of Meijer stores, compared with matched stores without singles, outside the holiday season, using Meijer's store-level sales.

1 — Who is comparable to Meijer

Every grocery account with BUILT singles (Kroger, Publix, Albertsons, Hy-Vee, Giant Eagle, Wegmans and others) carried singles before 4-packs, so none shows what happens when singles join an established 4-pack set. Target and Walmart did exactly that, and like Meijer they are supercenters.

2 — How Target and Walmart run singles

  • Target: singles in about 10% of stores in early October 2025, about 70% on average in November–December (53–83% week to week, including a holiday flavor), back to about 16% from January to May, then 82–91% from June to September 2026.
  • Walmart: about 40% of stores in January–March 2026, fading gradually (26% in April, 9% in July), then about 37% again in August–September.
  • The stores that keep singles in the off-season are self-selected strong stores, so their higher sales per store are not a benchmark for a full rollout.

3 — Did singles take sales from 4-packs?

One season can be measured: Target's 2025 holiday program, compared with the weeks just before and after it, with the same 4-pack items at grocery retailers removing BUILT-wide seasonality.

6 — How to test it at Meijer

  • Put 3–4 singles at the checkouts in a set of test stores (for example 40–60), and pick matched control stores with similar BUILT 4-pack sales and region.
  • Run at least 13 weeks, outside November–December, so holiday traffic does not blur the result.
  • Measure, test vs control: BUILT 4-pack base units per store per week (did 4-packs lose?), singles sold per store per week, and total BUILT bars and dollars per store.
  • This needs Meijer's store-level sales for the test and control stores. SPINS reports Meijer as one total, so it cannot show the difference between test and control stores.
  • Decide on total BUILT bars and dollars per store, not on 4-packs alone: some overlap is expected and is fine as long as the total grows.

Sources and caveats

  • SPINS weekly data, each retailer's own stores (RMA), through Sept 6, 2026. Prepared by Aevah with Mo.
  • SPINS does not record shelf location, so checkout and aisle placement cannot be separated anywhere in this add-on.
  • Target and Walmart are the only analogs, with one measurable season (Target, holidays 2025). Results describe what happened there; they are not proof of cause and not a forecast for Meijer.
  • The scenarios in section 5 multiply other retailers' sales per store by Meijer's store count; they are a sizing aid, and the store test is the way to measure Meijer itself.
  • Internal to BUILT and Aevah. Not for distribution outside BUILT.
