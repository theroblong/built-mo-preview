Meijer Account Brief

BUILT performance, 2026 launches, cannibalization and pricing at Meijer

Account: Meijer (Meijer's own 280 stores) · Channel: Food · Period: 13 weeks ending Sept 6, 2026 (June 14 – Sept 6), unless a table says otherwise · Data: SPINS weekly through Sept 6, 2026

Version 3 · issued October 16, 2026 · prepared by Aevah (Mo) for BUILT · combines the Oct 13 brief and its singles add-on

Summary

  • BUILT keeps growing at Meijer. BUILT sold 1.7 packages (base units) per Meijer store per week in late 2023 and 17.4 this quarter; its share of bar units went from 0.4% to 2.6%.
  • As the line widened, each item sells less per store: 5.6 packages per store per week at the Q2 2025 peak, with 3 main 4-packs, vs 3.7 now, with 5 main 4-packs plus 3 new flavors added in August.
  • The newest two 4-packs (Peanut Butter Cup and Cookies N Cream, April 2026) were mostly new business. In their first three months, for every 100 packages they sold, the older three 4-packs sold roughly 10–40 fewer than expected, so roughly 60–90% of the pair's sales were new to BUILT. Each older 4-pack gave up about 12–16% of its expected sales.
  • After the Cookie Dough launch in April 2025, the same pattern appeared and the older items' dip shrank after the first three months, so this first read may overstate the lasting effect.
  • Meijer's result is within the normal range for BUILT launches at other grocery (Food-channel) retailers. Target and Walmart (Mass) could not be measured, because their launches came during store expansions.
  • 4-count vs 1-count cannibalization cannot be measured yet. Meijer sells almost no singles, and too few launches elsewhere had both pack sizes on the shelf. A planned singles test at Meijer would answer it.
  • During promotions, BUILT's sales at Meijer respond about twice as strongly as the typical protein-bar competitor's. BUILT's regular price, $2.28 a bar, sits at the premium end with Barebells and Quest.

Not measurable yet. Meijer has no singles to compare against, and across all other retailers too few launches had both pack sizes on the shelf to give a reliable answer. The one clean case (a 4-pack launch at Publix in July 2024) drew a little from both pack sizes.

9 — Assortment recommendations (draft for discussion)

  • Keep all five main 4-packs. The newest two added mostly new sales; the older three each gave up about 12–16% of their expected sales in the first three months, and BUILT's total at Meijer still grew.
  • Give Rocky Road, Orange Cream Pop and Mint Chip time before judging them, and re-run this read when SPINS covers early December 2026. With eight 4-packs on shelf, expect sales per item to keep easing while the BUILT total grows; the question is whether the total grows faster than the overlap.
  • Singles: propose a check-lane test in a defined set of Meijer stores, with a matched set of stores without it, so the effect on the 4-pack set can be measured rather than assumed.
  • Promotions: BUILT's response to deals at Meijer is strong compared with the protein-bar set, so promotions are an effective volume lever. Regular price sits at the top of the set with Barebells and Quest.

10 — Singles at checkout: what other retailers show, and how to test it at Meijer

Singles: summary

  • Of the 16 grocery accounts in BUILT's SPINS data, Meijer is the only one that sells BUILT 4-packs with almost no singles today: about 8 single units over the last 13 weeks (one item, in one store), against about 68,500 4-packs. Meijer started selling BUILT in October 2023 with two 4-packs and singles of the same two flavors; the singles were in about 15–20 of its roughly 270 stores until late April 2024, then dropped within a few weeks to one to three stores (section 10.1). Every other grocer in the data that sells 4-packs had singles first (data from January 2023). Among the grocery and mass retailers in the data (military commissaries aside), the only ones that later expanded singles to 10% or more of their stores after 4-packs were already on the shelf are Walmart and Target; like Meijer, both sell groceries alongside general merchandise.
  • Both run singles as seasonal programs: in most stores for a few months, then back to a core of stores. Target put singles in roughly 70% of its stores for the 2025 holidays and in most stores again this summer.
  • When Target added singles for the 2025 holidays, most of the singles bars were additional sales for BUILT. Its 4-packs sold somewhat less than expected, but the size cannot be pinned down because Target added four new 4-pack flavors at the same time. Depending on the comparison, between none and just under half of the singles bars came out of 4-packs.
  • A typical grocer sells about 3.3 singles per item per store per week, a typical convenience chain about 2.0 (Wawa 4.5), and Target about 4.8 with singles in most stores. Singles sell for about 13% more per bar than 4-packs.
  • At Meijer, 4 checkout singles in all stores could sell roughly 2,200–5,400 bars a week (scenarios below). In these scenarios, even if close to half came out of 4-packs, BUILT would gain bars and dollars. This is a scenario, not a forecast.
  • Recommended: a 13-week test in a set of Meijer stores, compared with matched stores without singles, outside the holiday season, using Meijer's store-level sales.

10.1 — Meijer's own singles, 2023–24

Meijer has carried BUILT singles before: two items, Brownie Batter and Coconut (1.41 oz), the same flavors as its 4-packs, from October 2023 to June 2024. They sold about 2.6 units per item per store selling per week at regular price. In the same months a typical grocer sold about 4.0 and a typical convenience chain about 1.6; Walmart sold about 2.6, and Target had no singles yet. At regular price, singles cost about 15% more per bar than Meijer's 4-packs (about 20% for Brownie Batter, 9% for Coconut); at the prices shoppers actually paid, including promotions, the gap was about 5%.

Did they take sales from 4-packs? This cannot be measured from SPINS. Singles were about 2% of Meijer's 4-pack sales counted in bars, or about 8% counted in packs (the most possible if every single bought replaced a whole 4-pack). Each single was in at most 25 stores, and SPINS does not show which stores carried them. Meijer's 4-pack sales were still building after launch (about 1,500 rising to about 4,000 bars a week) and moved about 8% in a typical week, so any effect on 4-packs cannot be separated out in Meijer's account totals. The store test in this section is the way to measure it.

Who is comparable to Meijer

Every grocery account with BUILT singles (Kroger, Publix, Albertsons, Hy-Vee, Giant Eagle, Wegmans and others) carried singles before 4-packs, so none shows what happens when singles join an established 4-pack set. Target and Walmart did exactly that, and like Meijer, they sell groceries alongside general merchandise.

How Target and Walmart run singles

  • Target: singles in about 10% of stores in early October 2025, about 70% on average in November–December (53–83% week to week, including a holiday flavor), back to about 16% from February to May, then 82–91% from June to September 2026.
  • Walmart: about 40% of stores in February–March 2026, fading gradually (26% in April, 9% in July), then about 37% again in August–September.
  • The stores that keep singles in the off-season are self-selected strong stores, so their higher sales per store are not a benchmark for a full rollout.

Did singles take sales from 4-packs?

One season can be measured: Target's 2025 holiday program, compared with the weeks just before and after it, with the same 4-pack items at grocery retailers removing BUILT-wide seasonality.

How to test it at Meijer

  • Put 3–4 singles at the checkouts in a set of test stores (the number to be set from Meijer's store-level sales so the test can detect a 5–10% change in 4-pack sales), and pick matched control stores with similar BUILT 4-pack sales and region.
  • Keep the 4-pack lineup and promotions the same in test and control stores; new flavors are why Target's result is uncertain. Compare with at least 13 weeks before the test.
  • Run at least 13 weeks, outside November–December, so holiday traffic does not blur the result.
  • Measure, test vs control: BUILT 4-pack non-promoted units per store per week (did 4-packs lose?), singles sold per store per week, and total BUILT bars and dollars per store.
  • This needs Meijer's store-level sales for the test and control stores. SPINS reports Meijer as one total, so it cannot show the difference between test and control stores.
  • Decide on total BUILT bars and dollars per store, not on 4-packs alone: some overlap is expected and is fine as long as the total grows.
