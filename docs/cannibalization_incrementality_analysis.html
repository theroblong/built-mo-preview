The Critical Distinction
Not all cannibalization is the same — competitive displacement is the goal, not the problem
Mo's cannibalization model scores every new BUILT SKU against both own-brand siblings and competitor SKUs. The model's "Cannibalizing" status means something is being displaced — but the relevant question for your business is what is being displaced. A new BUILT flavor stealing demand from a competitor is market share growth. A new BUILT flavor stealing from your existing shelf SKUs is portfolio reshuffling. Mo separates these automatically. Own-brand donor count is the diagnostic number: 0 means new territory, 1+ means investigate which sibling is overlapping.
New territory
Own-brand donors: 0
No existing BUILT SKU conflicts. Any cannibalization is from competitors. This is share growth, not portfolio reshuffling.
→ Approve without swapping
Format / sibling overlap
Own-brand donors: 1–2, settling < 10%
Minor own-brand overlap (e.g. 4-pack entering where single-serve lives). Often reflects a planned format upgrade.
→ Approve; monitor the sibling SKU's trajectory
Portfolio conflict
Own-brand donors: multiple, rate stays > 20%
Flavor occupies space already served by existing BUILT SKUs. Net portfolio volume may not increase even if the new flavor sells.
→ Proactively offer a swap; or hold for data

Application to Dreamwich
What Aevah will tell the sales team for each Dreamwich flavor × account conversation
Once Dreamwich SKUs appear in SPINS data, Mo scores each one automatically on every weekly refresh — no setup required. The diagnostic is immediate: own-brand donor count and the cannibalization rate trajectory over the first 4–8 weeks. Flavors entering genuinely new taste territory (like the sour/fruit expansion shown above) will show 0 own-brand donors — a clean signal to bring to the buyer without offering a swap. Flavors adjacent to existing BUILT formats will surface their own-brand overlap by name and probability, letting the rep proactively decide whether to propose a trade-out before the buyer does.

The account-level variation shown above — the same flavor settling at different rates at Kroger vs. Walmart — is also actionable: it tells the sales team which accounts are ready for expansion and which need more time.
