New Flavor Incrementality — When Is Adding a New Flavor Not Advised?
Two BUILT launches scored across Kroger + Walmart · Real data from Aevah's cannibalization model · Own-brand vs. competitive displacement distinguished
The Diagnostic Framework
Aevah scores every new SKU against both own-brand siblings and competitor SKUs. The critical question is not whether cannibalization is occurring — it always is at launch — but who is being displaced. A new BUILT flavor stealing from competitors is market share growth. A new BUILT flavor stealing from its own siblings is portfolio reshuffling. Own-brand donor count is the primary diagnostic. Rate trajectory is the confirmation.
Incremental
Own-brand donors: 0 · Rate settles <10%
New flavor enters genuinely new demand territory. Competitive displacement is occurring — which is the goal. No existing BUILT SKU is materially affected.
→ Approve distribution without offering a swap
Watch
Own-brand donors: 1–2 · Rate settles 10–25%
Some own-brand overlap exists. May reflect a planned format upgrade or a sibling SKU being phased out. Monitor the sibling's trajectory before committing to full distribution.
→ Approve; track sibling SKU velocity closely
Cannibalizing
Own-brand donors: multiple · Rate stays >25%
New flavor is primarily rearranging existing BUILT demand. Net portfolio volume will likely be flat or negative even if the new SKU posts strong individual numbers.
→ Proactively propose a swap or hold for more data

Application to Dreamwich + How This Becomes a Live Feature
What Aevah will tell the sales team for each Dreamwich flavor × account

Once Dreamwich SKUs appear in SPINS data, Aevah scores each one automatically on every weekly refresh — no setup required. The diagnostic the examples above demonstrate is the same one the model runs for every new SKU: own-brand donor count and the rate trajectory over the first 4–8 weeks. Flavors entering genuinely new taste territory (like the sour/fruit example above) show 0 own-brand donors immediately — a clean signal to bring to the buyer. Flavors adjacent to existing BUILT formats surface their own-brand overlap by name and probability, letting the rep proactively offer a trade-out before the buyer raises it.

The account-level difference shown above — the same flavor settling at different speeds at Kroger vs. Walmart — is also actionable: it tells the sales team which accounts are ready for distribution expansion and which need more runway.

To bring this into Mo as a live filterable view: The underlying data is already live in Druid and the Mo API already has all the endpoints needed. The next step is building a UI experience within the Brand & Launch module — a retailer selector + flavor picker that renders the rate chart, own-brand donor count, and verdict for any SKU × account combination on demand, updated each week when SPINS refreshes. The examples on this page are the template for what each card in that view would show.
