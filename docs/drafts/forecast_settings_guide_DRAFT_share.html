Forecast settings guide

Every setting behind the Mo demand forecast, written for business analysts, FP&A and accounting: what it does, what it is set to, what happened when we changed it, and whether anyone should touch it.

Internal draft. Not yet cleared to share with BUILT. Values checked against the code on 12 Oct 2026 and reviewed twice by an independent check. No absolute accuracy figures appear here; test results are shown as directions or differences. Older test = run before the 7 Oct 2026 backtest fix (indicative only). Recent dates = the 6 forecasts made Dec 2025 to May 2026 (exploratory, too few for firm conclusions).

Two models, one engine

Both models use LightGBM: thousands of small decision trees, each one correcting the errors of the trees before it. Both use the same 56 inputs (recent sales, store count, price, promotions, seasonality and more). They differ only in how they reach weeks 2 to 13.

Recursive modelPredicts next week, feeds that prediction back in as if it were real, and repeats. Smooth shape; mistakes can compound.
Direct modelA separate model for each week ahead, each jumping straight from today. No compounding. This is what production serves today.

A2Maximum trees, and when to stop

Leave
What it does
The model keeps adding trees until it stops improving on a held-back 13-week check period, or hits the cap. "Patience" is how many trees in a row may fail to improve. For the recursive model the check scores one-week-ahead predictions made with actual recent sales, so the stopping point is not tuned to 13-week accuracy.
Current
recursive cap 6,000, patience 50 · direct cap 3,000, patience 100
What we found
10 of 18 test dates stopped at or just below 6,000. Raising the cap to 20,000 let 9 of the 18 train further, but forecasts barely moved (median change 0.01%). The direct cap has never been tested.
Advice
Leave.

A4Random seed

Fix planned
What it does
Training has a random element (A3's sampling). The seed fixes it, so a run repeats exactly on the same computer. Different computers differ slightly even with the same seed, so every comparison uses one machine.
Current
42 in both
What we found
Retraining the direct model with another seed moved item-level forecasts by a median of 10-11.5%. Averaging 5 seeds gave a small, consistent improvement.
Advice
Not a slider. Serve the average of several seeds rather than one.

A5Check period and final retraining

Fix planned
What it does
The last 13 weeks are held back to decide when to stop adding trees. The model should then be retrained on everything, including those weeks.
Current
The recursive model retrains on everything. The direct model as served does not: its training data ends at the start of its check period (7 Jun 2026), so it never learned from the most recent quarter. It also counts "8 weeks ahead" as 8 rows of data, which lands on the wrong calendar week wherever an item has missing weeks. The "fixed direct" model corrects both.
What we found
The fixed version (5 seeds averaged) scored better at item level in 2025, on the recent dates and overall.
Advice
Fix with the next model switch (C1).

A6Weight on recent weeks

Possible slider
What it does
Recent weeks count more in training. Each week's weight is e−0.02 × weeks ago: 26 weeks ago counts 59%, a year ago 35%, two years ago 12%.
Current
0.02 in both
What we found
Recursive model only
0, 0.01 and 0.04 all scored a little worse than 0.02 (0.1-0.2 points at item level). Only 0.01 passed the significance test, which with no pattern across the values is most likely training noise. The direct model's value has never been varied.
Advice
A possible slider ("how fast should the model forget?"), but there is no reason to move it today.

A7Low, central and high forecasts

Measure first
What it does
Each model gives three forecasts per item, per retailer, per week: a low (10th percentile), a central (50th) and a high (90th). It learns on a log scale so large and small items are treated fairly.
Read with care
  • Ranges are per item, per week. Adding up item highs does not give a portfolio high; items rarely all run high at once.
  • New items (B5) get a fixed ±45% range around last week, not a learned one.
  • Lapsed items on the direct model get a range from zero to their full expected level.
Current
The recursive model stretches its high forecast by ×1.0059. That factor was set in May 2026 on an older model version and should be re-derived at every retrain. The direct model has no stretch.
What we found
How often actuals land outside the current models' ranges has not been measured.
Advice
Measure range coverage before showing ranges to BUILT as "80% ranges".

A8Inputs held at forecast-date values

Leave
What it does
Recent-sales trend inputs and store count are kept at their forecast-date values for all 13 weeks. Price is held at the last observed price.
What we found
  • Letting the sales-trend inputs update each week, from the model's own predictions, made forecasts much worse (about 12 points at item level). Holding them is a deliberate brake.
  • Older test Store count made little difference even when the test used the actual future store counts (under 1 point).
Advice
Leave. Known store-count changes (expansion plans, new retailers) belong in planning inputs, not here.

B2Seasonal index

Under review
What it does
Both models see the week of the year as an input. In addition, for items without a usable same-week-last-year (B3), the recursive model nudges each week by the change in an average seasonal index from the week before. Items with a usable year-ago week get their seasonality from B3 instead.
What we found
  • Earlier tests that favored this method used a seasonal index built with data from after the forecast date (a look-ahead).
  • Older test Rebuilt using only data available at each forecast date, the seasonal step did worse than switching it off: about 2 points worse at item level and slightly worse at account level. Portfolio totals looked better only because errors in different item age groups cancelled; within each age group it was the same or worse. Most of the item-level harm came from new items, where the index is now switched off.
Advice
The evidence so far says it does not help. It stays until a dedicated test on the current setup decides.

B3Seasonal pull toward last year

Keep 10%
What it does
Recursive model
How much the forecast leans toward "the same week last year, scaled by this item's growth" instead of the model's own projection.
Current
10%
What we found
0%, 5%, 10%, 20% and 30% were all close. At 30%:
  • Forecasts came out a little lower in 2025 (1-5%) and up to 3% higher in 2026; Oct-Dec 2025 over-forecasting was smaller.
  • Week-by-week item forecasts were about 1 point worse.
  • Monthly totals improved in 4 of 5 item age groups, but only on the recent dates and not significantly. Over all 18 dates they were slightly worse.
  • Established items (52+ weeks), the largest group by volume, got worse at every level.
Advice
Keep 10%. More pull has no proven benefit today and hurts established items.

B4Growth cap for the seasonal pull

Needs test
What it does
Recursive model
The "growth" in B3 compares one recent week with the same week a year earlier (not year-to-date). That ratio is capped between half and double.
Current
0.5 to 2.0
What we found
  • Removing the cap looked very harmful, but almost all the harm came from about 116 item-date combinations whose year-ago week was before the item was really in stores: a handful of units, so growth looked like 200× to 1,375×. The cap binds for 59% of items.
  • A version that also skipped the comparison when the item was in too few stores a year ago (and measured growth per store) removed most of that harm, with no overall accuracy change.
  • The "year ago" week is actually 51 weeks back (an off-by-one). Fixing it made no difference.
Advice
Not a slider. Replacing the cap with the store-count rule needs one more test: that rule alone, with nothing else changed.

B5New items (under 13 weeks of sales)

Under review
What it does
The recursive model does not forecast items with under 13 weeks of history at a retailer. They get last week's sales carried forward, with a fixed ±45% range. The direct model forecasts them like any other item.
What we found
  • Older test Carrying the last value forward won in every age group and on 3 of 4 test dates against the alternatives tried.
  • Over all 18 test dates (Dec 2024 to May 2026) the rule beat the 5-seed direct model on these items at every level. That lead comes from 2025: on the recent dates the direct model was better at item and portfolio level and tied at account level. Date by date, the rule won 10 of 18 and none of the 5 dates in 2026.
  • Adding the seasonal index to new items made them worse because of a defect that inflated them by 10-30%. It is switched off.
  • The ±45% width has never been tested.
Advice
Keep for now. The recent dates favor letting the direct model forecast new items, which the next model decision (C1) would settle.

B6Lapsed items (9 weeks without sales)

Needs test
What it does
After 9 weeks with no sales at a retailer, an item counts as lapsed. The recursive model forecasts zero. The direct model forecasts the chance it comes back times its expected level. In older data, 365 of 878 lapses resumed, at about 1.06× their old level.
Current
9 weeks
What we found
9 weeks sits at a natural gap in the data: very few items go silent for 5-8 weeks and come back. Other thresholds have not been tested.
Advice
A possible slider ("how long before we stop expecting sales?") after a test.

C1Model choice and combination

Rule decides
What it does
Which model production serves, or how two models are averaged (for example 50% recursive + 50% fixed direct).
Current
The direct model as served, with the A5 flaws.
What we found
Recent dates, exploratory
  • The fixed direct model (5 seeds averaged) is the only candidate ahead of the served model at account × month, but it won on 4 of 6 dates; the rule needs 5.
  • The 50/50 combination beat last value carried forward on all 6 dates, but is slightly behind the served model at account × month.
  • The recursive model alone trails last value.
  • No candidate has passed the pre-registered decision rule yet. Saved forecasts become scorable when SPINS data reaches 6 Dec 2026.
Advice
Not a slider. Aevah shows which model is serving, why, and how it compares with L4W velocity × stores and with last value carried forward.

Group D

What the forecast covers

Units
SPINS base units: SPINS's modeled sales without promotions. Promotion lift is not in the base forecast. SPINS restates past weeks by up to 35 weeks back.
Dollars
Forecast units × the last observed price, held flat.
Not covered
Item-retailer combinations that don't exist yet (new distribution), roughly 14% of a quarter's volume. These need planning inputs.
Scope
Military accounts excluded. Alaska and Hawaii dropped. Regional markets (RMA) used rather than the overlapping CRMA geographies.
Model age
The served direct model's training data ends 7 Jun 2026.
